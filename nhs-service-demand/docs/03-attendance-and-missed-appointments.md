# 3. Attendance and missed appointments

*Report Figs 4.1–4.4. Notebook section C, 1.1. Example: [`03_attendance.py`](../examples/03_attendance.py).*

Across all appointments with a known status, **95.6% were attended and 4.4%
missed**. The question is where the misses concentrate.

## By mode and professional (Figs 4.1–4.2)

| | % missed (DNA) |
|---|---|
| Telephone | **2.0** |
| Video/Online | 4.6 |
| Home Visit | 4.9 |
| Face-to-Face | **5.8** |
| GP | **2.6** |
| Other practice staff | **6.5** |

Telephone appointments are hardly ever missed, plausibly because the practice
calls the patient.
Appointments with other practice staff are missed more than twice as often as
those with GPs. That partly reflects what those appointments are, as the next
section shows: more of them are booked far ahead.

## By booking lead time (Fig 4.3)

| Booked | % missed |
|---|---|
| Same day | **1.8** |
| 1 day | 4.0 |
| 2–7 days | 5.9 |
| 8–14 days | 7.6 |
| 15–21 days | 8.4 |
| 22–28 days | 8.8 |
| More than 28 days | **10.9** |

This is the clearest pattern in the data. **The further ahead an appointment
is booked, the more likely it is to be missed**: six times as likely beyond
28 days as on the same day. Patients forget, or their need passes.

## Over time (Fig 4.4)

- Monthly attendance stayed between **94.6% and 97.1%**.
- Volume dropped sharply in **April–May 2020** (14.5M and 15.4M
  appointments), the first COVID-19 lockdown. It then recovered, with
  seasonal dips.

## What it led to

Two recommendations follow from the lead-time pattern:
- **reminders**, aimed at appointments booked weeks ahead;
- **more same-day capacity**, since same-day appointments are almost always
  kept.
