# 8. Observations and recommendations

*Report section 5 and Appendix 1. Example: [`08_summary_check.py`](../examples/08_summary_check.py).*

## Observations (Table 5.1)

| Observation | What the data shows |
|---|---|
| High, consistent attendance | 94–97% attended in every region and month; DNA at most 6% |
| GPs carry the urgent work | GPs deliver 51% overall, 72% of telephone and 68% of same-day appointments |
| Face-to-face and telephone dominate | Face-to-face 54–68%, telephone 31–45% by region; home visits and video at most 2% |
| Appointments are short | 1–10 minutes is 47–52% of recorded durations in every region |
| Lead time drives missed appointments | DNA rises from 1.8% (same day) to 10.9% (over 28 days) |
| Same-day demand is large | Same-day is 41–50% of bookings in every region |
| Regions differ in degree, not kind | Top and bottom five overlap on every measure |
| Alternative settings are under-used | PCN 1.9–2.8% and Extended Access 0.7–0.8% of appointments, rising slowly |
| Routine consultations dominate | 37–41% of the five main categories each month |

## Recommendations (Tables 5.2–5.3)

| Recommendation | Grounded in |
|---|---|
| **Communication and reminders** to reduce missed appointments, aimed at bookings made weeks ahead | DNA rises six-fold with lead time ([3](03-attendance-and-missed-appointments.md)) |
| **Expand remote options:** telephone and video for flexibility | Telephone DNA is 2.0% vs 5.8% face-to-face ([3](03-attendance-and-missed-appointments.md)); public sentiment towards telehealth is positive ([7](07-twitter-hashtags-and-sentiment.md)) |
| **Optimise GP and staff use:** give other staff more of the future, non-urgent bookings and free GPs for urgent work | GPs hold 68% of same-day work; other staff 76% of long-lead work ([4](04-staff-mode-and-lead-time.md)) |
| **Increase urgent-care capacity:** more same-day face-to-face and telephone slots | Same-day is the largest booking category everywhere, and the best attended |
| **Use Primary Care Networks and Extended Access:** move routine and planned work there | About 3% of appointments, but growing ([5](05-duration-and-national-categories.md)) |
| **Staff for the peaks:** temporary slots in high-demand months | March 2022 was the busiest month in every duration band ([5](05-duration-and-national-categories.md)) |
| **Support community and elderly care:** care-home assessments and home visits | Home visits and care-home work are small categories and vary between regions (appendix Fig 5.6) |
| **Healthcare communication via social media:** use high-traffic hashtags; promote digital health | [7](07-twitter-hashtags-and-sentiment.md) |

## The report's figures against the data

The example recomputes every figure in Table 5.1 and sets it beside the
report's wording. Most match exactly.

Three summary lines differ. The table above uses the corrected values:
- **Same-day bookings:** "20.5–21.8%" is actually 41–50%. The report's
  20.5–21.8% is the share booked 2–7 days ahead.
- **GP share in the top five:** "43.8–56.2%" is actually 44.7–57.6%.
- **Routine consultations:** "40–45% of monthly appointments" is actually
  37–41%.

The regional appendix has a few more of the same kind, listed in
[6](06-regional-comparison.md). All of them come from reading values off
stacked charts with many similarly coloured segments. The charts
themselves are right.

**The same-day correction strengthens a recommendation.** Same-day demand is
nearly half of all appointments, not a fifth, and it is the best-attended
booking type. That makes **increasing urgent, same-day capacity** the
recommendation with the most support in the data.

## Limitations

- **Data quality:**
  - GP systems are not built for analysis, and recording practice varies;
  - a quarter of appointments have no usable duration.
- **Different periods:** the three appointment files cover 30, 11 and 7
  months, so the views cannot be combined into one model.
- **Counts, not people:** the data counts appointments, not patients. A rise
  in appointments could be more patients or the same patients more often.
- **Tweets:** a single day, and not UK-specific.
- **Descriptive only:** the analysis shows associations, such as lead time
  with DNA, but not causes. Reminders should be piloted and measured before
  being rolled out.
