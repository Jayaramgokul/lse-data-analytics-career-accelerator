# 6. Who engages, and which channels work

*Report insights A–B. Examples: [`04_key_metrics_and_advertising.py`](../examples/04_key_metrics_and_advertising.py), [`05_engagement_by_segment.py`](../examples/05_engagement_by_segment.py).*

## The most engaged customers: counts and rates

The report's profile of the most engaged customer is **high-earning, married,
Spanish, aged 45–65, with a graduate degree, buying mostly beverages**.

Read from the dashboard's counts, this profile is also simply **the largest
group in every attribute**:

| Group | Share of all customers | Share of engaged customers |
|---|---|---|
| Spain | 49.3% | 49.9% |
| Married | 38.7% | 40.5% |
| Graduation | 50.4% | 46.5% |
| Aged 45–65 | 54.5% | 54.7% |

Each group holds about the same share of engaged customers as of customers
overall. So **counts find the biggest segment, not the most engaged one**.
Rates per customer separate behaviour from size:

| Group | Spend per customer | Campaign response |
|---|---|---|
| High income | **$1,262** | **23.1%** |
| Low income | $105 | 11.6% |
| Single · Divorced · Widowed | $613 · $611 · $728 | **22.5% · 20.7% · 23.7%** |
| Married · Together | $591 · $609 | 11.4% · 10.5% |
| No children | **$1,105** | **26.5%** |
| Teenagers at home | $704 | 8.2% |
| PhD | $677 | 21.0% |

On rates:
- **High income** stands out on every measure.
- **The report's second observation is confirmed and becomes stronger:**
  single people and households without children responded most to recent
  marketing (about twice the rate of married customers and three times that
  of households with teenagers). The report suggested product relevance as
  the reason.

Country makes little difference per customer. Spain's response (16.1%) is
close to the average (15.0%).

## Advertising channels

| Channel | Conversions | Engaged customers | On-the-fence | Ready-to-buy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Twitter | **164** | 27 | 127 | 37 |
| Bulk mail | 163 | 27 | **145** | 18 |
| Instagram | 162 | 26 | 109 | **53** |
| Facebook | 142 | 21 | 93 | 49 |
| Brochure | 30 | 6 | 11 | 19 |

- **Overall:** Twitter, bulk mail and Instagram are effectively tied. Facebook
  is slightly behind, and brochures far behind. The tie explains a mismatch
  on the dashboard:
  - the treemap ranks Twitter first;
  - the "Best Advertising Channel" tile, computed on engaged customers, shows
    Bulkmail, where the two tie at 27.
- **By propensity:** this is the more useful split, and it supports the
  report's observation that high-propensity customers prefer Instagram.
  - **Ready-to-buy** customers converted most through **Instagram and
    Facebook**, and least through bulk mail.
  - **On-the-fence** customers converted most through **bulk mail and
    Twitter.**

## Website and marketing conversion

The report noted that **average website conversion outruns marketing
conversion**: 77.8% against 30% on the dashboard's tiles. The two rates
measure different things:
- web purchases per website visit;
- the share of customers who accepted the campaign.

So the comparison shows that the website turns visits into sales well. It
does not show that one channel beats the other. Across all customers the
figures are 76.8% and 15.0%.

## What follows from this

- **Target high-income, single and childless customers.** They spend most
  and respond most.
- **Match the channel to the customer:**
  - Instagram and Facebook for customers already likely to buy;
  - bulk mail and Twitter for those on the fence.
- **Drop or rethink brochures.**
